# Board: characters

- **Figma link:** (file `characters figma.svg`, exported 2026-11-05; link to add)
- **Date collected:** 2026-11-05
- **Collected by:** the team
- **What this board is about:** five classic feature-animation characters that are household objects with faces: a branched candle holder, a mantel clock, a wardrobe, a footstool and a teapot with a cup. They show how an ordinary object becomes a character.
- **Exports (not committed):** `exports/characters_figma.svg` (the whole board), `exports/char_01.png` to `char_05.png` (the five images extracted from it).

## The rule for this board

We study the **design logic**. We do not use the characters. Not their faces, proportions, costumes, ornaments, colour schemes or poses, and not their names or the film's name in any prompt. Our objects stay minimal and scribbly (dot eyes, a small smile), so they do not look like these. See the distance rules at the bottom.

## Frames

### R01 · branched candle holder (`exports/char_01.png`)
- **What we take (qualities, our own words):**
  - face placement: the face sits on the one flat, wide part of the object, where it reads best
  - limbs: thin arms grow out of the object's own form and end in the object's own parts (the candle cups), so nothing is added that the object does not already have
  - the light source is part of the character: the flames are what it "feels"
  - colour: warm gold and cream with a strong highlight shape
  - pose: asymmetric arms give a gesture and a clear silhouette
- **What we do not take:** the face, the arms, the tilted pose, the three flames, the gold finish.
- **Applies to:** `lamp_floor` (where the face sits, how a gesture is read), the lamp glow.
- **Prompt wording (qualities only):** "a household object whose face sits on its flattest wide surface, thin limbs that grow from the object's own parts, a warm light that is part of the character"
- **Status:** noted

### R02 · mantel clock (`exports/char_02.png`)
- **What we take:**
  - the object's own mechanism is the face and body: hands, pendulum and dial do the acting
  - a heavy-lidded, relaxed expression shows personality with very little drawing
  - a chunky, rounded body that is stable and symmetrical, with one gesturing hand
  - colour: warm browns and ochres with one lighter accent
- **What we do not take:** the face, the moustache-like shape, the ornaments, the colour set.
- **Applies to:** the wall clock (the sound plan's pulse), the `armchair` (a stable, solid silhouette).
- **Prompt wording:** "the object's own mechanism acts as its features, a chunky stable body, a relaxed heavy-lidded look"
- **Status:** noted

### R03 · wardrobe (`exports/char_03.png`)
- **What we take:**
  - scale: a tall, stately silhouette with a small face high on the form reads as dignified
  - the ornamental curves of the furniture double as clothing
  - colour: pale sage and cream with a gold accent
  - the object's parts (drawers, handles) become the body language
- **What we do not take:** the face, the headpiece, the colours, the drawer details.
- **Applies to:** `armchair` (tall, dignified), the cradle and crib (furniture curves as character).
- **Prompt wording:** "a tall furniture silhouette with a small face high on the form, ornamental curves that read as clothing"
- **Status:** noted

### R04 · footstool, seen from behind (`exports/char_04.png`)
- **What we take:**
  - **life without a face**: the character acts only through posture (a tassel that moves like a tail, legs that act like paws). This is exactly our rule that things act with posture
  - a rounded, heavy, cushioned body with a clear top and bottom
  - rich red with gold trim as a way to make a plain object feel precious
- **What we do not take:** the dog-like tail and paws, the red and gold trim, the pose.
- **Applies to:** `side_table` (its curly legs as feet), `armchair` and the cradle (acting by posture, not by face).
- **Prompt wording:** "an object with no face that acts through posture, a part of it moving like a tail, legs that act like paws"
- **Status:** noted. Most useful frame for us.

### R05 · teapot with a cup (`exports/char_05.png`)
- **What we take:**
  - a **relationship** between a big object and a small one, shown by one glance upward
  - the object's spout and lid act as parts of the mouth and head
  - bright accent colours, used sparingly against a plain body
- **What we do not take:** the large eyes with lashes, the eyebrows, the colours, the lid ornament, the pairing itself.
- **Applies to:** `mug` and `side_table` (a small thing looking up at a bigger thing), the Teddy Bear and the cushion.
- **Prompt wording:** "a small object looking up at a larger one, the object's own spout or handle acting as part of the face"
- **Status:** noted

## What we take for our whole look (across the board)

1. **Faces live in the object's form**, not on top of it.
2. **Limbs are the object's own parts**, kept minimal.
3. **Posture carries the performance**, and some characters need no face at all.
4. **A clear silhouette**: you can read each character in one glance.
5. **A strong highlight shape** on a flat colour, instead of soft shading.
6. **Relationships between objects** (big and small, looking at each other) make the room feel alive.

## Distance rules (so our designs stay ours)

These characters are famous, and our idea is close. Keep the distance:

- **Keep our minimal faces**: two dots and a small smile. No eyelashes, no eyebrows, no large shiny eyes, no teeth.
- **No gloved hands, no fingers, no hats or costumes.** Our objects do not have hands.
- **Keep our palette** (eight colours, mostly cold with one warm gold). Do not use candy colours.
- **Keep our scribbly outline** (thin, brown, irregular). Do not switch to a clean, thick vector outline.
- **Our objects are mostly passive.** They act with small movements: a lean, a droop, a blink. They do not talk, sing or dance.
- **Never use these images as references for the generator.** Describe qualities only.
