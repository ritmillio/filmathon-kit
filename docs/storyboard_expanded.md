# Expanded Storyboard – 60-Second Film

## Overall concept

Tell the story of an entire human life through the perspective of the objects that inhabit one room.

**The humans are never shown. The objects are the witnesses.**

The room becomes a living world. It changes as its owner grows up, leaves, builds a family, grows old, and eventually disappears. The objects experience these changes without fully understanding them. They have their own personalities, relationships, routines, and emotional lives.

**Format:** 60-second poetic, slightly magical, emotional animation.

### Visual direction
- **Location:** One room throughout the film.
- **Main perspective:** The objects' world, at their eye level.
- **Humans:** Never visible. Their existence is communicated through changing furniture, toys, drawings, sounds, movement, and traces of everyday life.
- **Animation style:** Expressive, tactile, slightly surreal, cinematic.
- **Storytelling:** No dialogue necessary. The story should be understandable through movement, lighting, sound, and objects.
- **Emotional journey:** Birth → Playfulness → Rebellion → Separation → Family → Silence → Old age → Goodbye.

The room itself is almost another character. It grows older alongside the objects.

---

## Scene 1 — BIRTH (0–6 seconds)

**Stage:** Birth  
**Key moment:** The Lamp opens its eye at the first cry.

### Story purpose
The beginning of life. The room wakes up, and the Lamp becomes the first character to experience the world.

### Detailed action
1. Start with a completely dark screen.
2. A faint mechanical click is heard.
3. A tiny warm light appears in the darkness.
4. The Lamp flickers twice, as if it is trying to understand what is happening.
5. Its eye slowly opens for the first time.
6. The light grows stronger, illuminating a small part of the room.
7. Dust particles become visible in the beam of light.
8. The Lamp looks around, slightly confused, then develops its characteristic smile.
9. A faint baby cry can be heard from outside the frame.
10. The Lamp reacts to the sound with a little jump of excitement.

### Additional ideas
- The lampshade could shake slightly when the Lamp first wakes up.
- Its light could pulse in rhythm with the distant cry.
- The first thing it sees could be a small mobile hanging above the room, gently rotating.
- The Lamp tries to turn its head in different directions, discovering its surroundings.

### Camera
Extreme close-up on the Lamp's eye, followed by a slow pullback revealing the room.

### Sound design
A soft click, electrical hum, gentle flickering, distant baby cry, delicate musical beginning.

### Transition
The light from the Lamp becomes a visual match cut into the bright, colourful childhood room.

---

## Scene 2 — CHILDHOOD (6–14 seconds)

**Stage:** Childhood  
**Key moment:** Toys hop and play; height marks climb.

### Story purpose
The room becomes full of life. We see childhood through the objects, toys, and traces of a growing child.

### Detailed action
1. The camera reveals a brighter, more colourful room.
2. Toys are scattered across the floor.
3. A small toy car suddenly rolls across the room by itself.
4. A stuffed toy bounces slightly, as if laughing.
5. Building blocks tumble over and rearrange themselves.
6. The Lamp jumps on one leg, moving around the room with excitement.
7. It switches itself on and off playfully, creating little flashes of light.
8. On the wall, pencil height marks appear and gradually climb higher.
9. We see several dates or small drawings next to the marks, suggesting time passing.
10. A drawing slowly appears on the wall, perhaps a house, a sun, or a family.
11. The Bird (which will become important later) is still somewhere low in the room, among the toys.

### Additional ideas
- A toy train could circle the room, passing the Lamp several times.
- The Lamp tries to follow the train but cannot quite keep up.
- A ball rolls into the Lamp, making it stumble backwards.
- The teddy bear could sit on the floor and watch the chaos.
- A paper airplane flies across the frame and lands on the windowsill.
- The height marks could change through several quick visual jumps, showing years passing without needing to show a human.

### Camera
Dynamic, low-angle tracking shots following the toys. Quick cuts between playful details.

### Sound design
Toy rattles, rolling wheels, wooden blocks falling, playful music, little electrical clicks from the Lamp.

### Transition
The cheerful music suddenly cuts. A door slams somewhere outside the frame.

---

## Scene 3 — TEENS (14–22 seconds)

**Stage:** Teens  
**Key moment:** Posters, a slammed door; the Bird is moved to a high shelf.

### Story purpose
The atmosphere changes. The room becomes more personal, more chaotic, and slightly rebellious. The objects begin to experience neglect.

### Detailed action
1. The colours of the room become stronger and slightly darker.
2. The childhood drawings are replaced by posters.
3. New posters appear on the walls in a quick montage.
4. Books and clothes are scattered around.
5. The toys that were previously moving around happily are now pushed aside.
6. The Lamp is switched on late at night.
7. It looks around, confused by the sudden change in its environment.
8. A door slams offscreen.
9. The vibration makes objects on the shelves shake.
10. The Bird is suddenly no longer in its familiar place.
11. Through a visual transition, we discover it sitting alone on a high shelf.
12. It looks down at the room, unable to join the other objects.
13. The Lamp notices the Bird and tries to look up at it.
14. The Bird remains still.

### Additional ideas
- A poster slowly covers an old childhood drawing.
- A toy car is left upside down under the bed.
- A football rolls into a corner and never moves again.
- The Lamp tries to cheer everyone up by flickering or dancing, but nobody responds.
- The Bird could attempt to flap its wings but realise it cannot reach the lower part of the room.
- A stack of books could grow taller, making the Bird feel even more isolated.

### Camera
Slightly more static and dramatic. Use reflections, silhouettes of objects, and close-ups.

### Sound design
Door slam, muffled music from outside the room, vibrating objects, a few isolated mechanical sounds.

### Transition
The music fades. The room becomes quiet. A suitcase begins to move into the frame.

---

## Scene 4 — AWAY (22–28 seconds)

**Stage:** Away  
**Key moment:** Things pack themselves; the Bird is alone, looking at the door.

### Story purpose
Separation. The room is being emptied. The Bird is left behind, waiting for someone who does not return immediately.

### Detailed action
1. Cardboard boxes appear around the room.
2. Books disappear from the shelves.
3. Posters are removed, revealing faded rectangles on the wall.
4. Clothes and toys are no longer scattered around.
5. The Lamp moves around the room, looking for familiar objects.
6. It tries to turn itself on, but the room feels strangely empty.
7. The Bird sits on the high shelf, watching everything.
8. It slowly turns its head towards the door.
9. The door opens slightly, allowing a beam of light to enter.
10. The Bird becomes excited and leans forward.
11. But the door closes again.
12. The Bird remains alone.
13. The Lamp's light becomes softer.

### Additional ideas
- A small forgotten toy rolls out from underneath the bed as everything is packed.
- The Bird tries to follow the moving boxes with its eyes.
- One box passes directly in front of the camera, creating a natural transition.
- A small piece of paper with a child's drawing remains stuck under a table.
- The room could feel physically larger as furniture disappears.
- The Bird could look towards the door every time it hears a sound from the hallway.

### Camera
Slow tracking shots. More negative space in the composition. The camera should emphasise how empty the room has become.

### Sound design
Cardboard sliding, tape being pulled, wheels rolling, distant footsteps outside the room, door closing.

### Transition
The last box leaves. A new bed slowly enters the frame.

---

## Scene 5 — FAMILY (28–38 seconds)

**Stage:** Family  
**Key moment:** Double bed, a crib; the Bird is lifted into the crib.

### Story purpose
Life begins again. The room is no longer a teenager's room. It becomes a shared space, and the objects witness the arrival of a new generation.

### Detailed action
1. The empty room gradually transforms.
2. A double bed appears.
3. New curtains, bedding, and furniture replace the old ones.
4. The lighting becomes warmer and softer.
5. A crib appears beside the bed.
6. A mobile hangs above it, slowly rotating.
7. The Bird is carefully relocated from the high shelf into the crib.
8. It initially looks confused by its new surroundings.
9. The mobile starts spinning, and the Bird follows it with its eyes.
10. A soft light from the Lamp illuminates the crib.
11. The Bird seems to become protective of the new space.
12. The room fills with small details: folded blankets, little toys, a night light, soft colours.
13. The Lamp stands nearby, watching over the crib.

### Additional ideas
- The Bird could rock gently as the crib moves.
- The mobile's hanging toys could dance above the Bird.
- The Lamp could dim its light whenever the room becomes quiet.
- The blanket could slowly rise and fall, suggesting someone sleeping underneath, without showing any human.
- A tiny sock could fall out of the crib.
- The Bird could gently tilt its head towards the crib's moving mobile.
- The Lamp and Bird could share a little moment: the Lamp smiles, and the Bird finally looks less lonely.

### Camera
Slow, elegant movements. A gentle orbit around the crib. Close-ups of the Bird, mobile, and Lamp.

### Sound design
Soft lullaby, gentle wooden creaking, mobile music, quiet room ambience.

### Transition
The mobile gradually slows down. Its colours fade. The crib begins to disappear.

---

## Scene 6 — QUIET YEARS (38–46 seconds)

**Stage:** Quiet years  
**Key moment:** The crib goes, the marks stop, colours soften.

### Story purpose
Time passes. The objects begin to understand that something has changed. The lively years are over.

### Detailed action
1. The crib is no longer there.
2. The mobile disappears.
3. The colourful blankets and toys are gone.
4. The room becomes simpler and quieter.
5. The height marks on the wall stop changing.
6. We see the last pencil mark, with an old date beside it.
7. The posters are faded.
8. The wallpaper has lost its colour.
9. The Bird is back on a shelf, but now it looks older and more fragile.
10. The Lamp still moves around the room, but much more slowly.
11. It switches on and off less frequently.
12. The room receives a long beam of afternoon sunlight.
13. Dust floats through the air.
14. The objects barely move.

### Additional ideas
- The old childhood drawing from Scene 2 could still be visible under faded wallpaper.
- A forgotten toy appears inside a drawer when it opens slightly.
- The Bird looks at the old height marks.
- The Lamp tries to jump on one leg, but lands awkwardly.
- A clock could visibly speed up through several time jumps.
- The shadows on the wall could move from morning to evening several times in a few seconds.

### Camera
Wide static composition. Slow push-ins. Longer shots and less movement.

### Sound design
Clock ticking, distant wind, soft floor creaks, very subtle electrical humming.

### Transition
A creaking sound. The old armchair slowly moves into the frame.

---

## Scene 7 — OLD AGE (46–54 seconds)

**Stage:** Old age  
**Key moment:** Armchair and Lamp lean toward the bed; everything trembles.

### Story purpose
The final chapter of the room's life. The owner has grown old. The objects have aged too, and they seem to sense that something is coming to an end.

### Detailed action
1. The room is now noticeably older.
2. The wallpaper is faded, the furniture has scratches, and the wooden floor creaks.
3. The old armchair stands next to the bed.
4. The Lamp is positioned close to the bed, giving off a warm but weaker light.
5. The Lamp looks towards the bed.
6. Its lampshade slowly tilts forward, as if trying to get closer.
7. The armchair shifts slightly towards the bed.
8. The Bird watches from its shelf.
9. A blanket on the bed moves very subtly, suggesting someone is resting underneath, but nobody is visible.
10. The Lamp flickers.
11. The room trembles slightly.
12. The shadows on the wall shake.
13. A small picture frame slowly tilts.
14. The Bird turns its head towards the Lamp.
15. The Lamp tries to stay illuminated, but its light becomes weaker.
16. Everything seems to hold its breath.

### Additional ideas
- The armchair could gently rock once, then stop.
- The Lamp could try to stand taller, as if refusing to give up.
- A tiny dust particle could fall from the lampshade.
- The Bird could slowly move closer to the edge of its shelf.
- The clock could stop ticking for a moment, then start again.
- The light from the Lamp could briefly illuminate an old childhood drawing still hanging on the wall.
- The room could experience a subtle visual tremor, with objects vibrating in slow motion.

### Particularly strong visual idea
The Lamp casts its light across the bed. In the illuminated area, we see faint traces of the entire life that happened in this room: the outline of a mobile, a childhood drawing, old height marks, and the shadow of the Bird.

For a brief moment, the whole history of the room seems to exist in one image.

### Camera
Slow dolly towards the bed. Close-up of the Lamp's eye, then the Bird looking towards it. Very gentle camera movement.

### Sound design
Old floorboards creaking, soft clock ticking, distant wind, electrical buzzing, a low musical note that gradually becomes quieter.

### Transition
The Lamp flickers once. Cut to darkness.

---

## Scene 8 — END (54–60 seconds)

**Stage:** End  
**Key moment:** Things go still one by one; the Lamp is last.

### Story purpose
Goodbye. The room falls silent. The objects that witnessed an entire lifetime finally become still.

### Detailed action
1. We return to the wider view of the bedroom.
2. Everything is completely still.
3. The Bird stops moving.
4. The armchair stops rocking.
5. The clock stops ticking.
6. The curtains stop moving.
7. The room becomes silent.
8. The Lamp remains illuminated.
9. It looks around one last time.
10. Its light gently flickers.
11. It seems to remember the colourful childhood, the toys, the posters, the crib, and all the years that passed.
12. Its smile remains.
13. The light slowly dims.
14. One final flicker.
15. Darkness.

### Additional ideas
- Before the Lamp goes out, its light could briefly illuminate the old height marks on the wall.
- The Bird could be visible in the very last moment, facing the Lamp.
- A tiny reflection of the Lamp's light could remain in the Bird's eye.
- The last thing we hear could be the familiar electrical click from the beginning, creating a circular narrative.
- Alternatively, the Lamp's light could go out, but a very faint glow could remain somewhere in the room, suggesting that memories never completely disappear.

### Preferred ending
The Lamp switches off.

We stay in darkness for one full second.

Then, very briefly, we hear the same tiny mechanical click from the beginning.

No light comes back.

Cut to black.

### Camera
Wide shot, completely static. No movement after the final flicker.

### Sound design
Music disappears. One electrical click. Silence.

## Recurring visual elements and continuity

Use a few recurring visual elements to connect the scenes and make the film feel like one continuous story rather than eight separate sequences.

| Recurring element | How it evolves |
|---|---|
| The Lamp | Starts energetic and curious, becomes playful, then protective, finally fragile |
| The Bird | Starts as a background object, becomes isolated, finds a new purpose, and witnesses the ending |
| The height marks | Appear during childhood, stop during the quiet years, remain as a memory |
| The door | Represents arrivals, departures, separation, and the passage of time |
| The lighting | Warm and bright → colourful → dramatic → empty → soft → faded → golden → darkness |
| The room | Grows, changes, becomes a family home, ages, and finally falls silent |

## Additional creative recommendations

### 1. Make the objects interact with each other

Don't let them simply exist in the room. Give them relationships:
- The Lamp tries to cheer up the Bird.
- The Bird watches the Lamp's movements.
- The Lamp illuminates objects that are important to the Bird.
- The armchair and Lamp become companions in old age.

### 2. Use visual match cuts to show time passing

For example:
- A spinning toy wheel transitions into a spinning mobile.
- A colourful childhood drawing transitions into its faded version.
- A moving curtain transitions into the same curtain, now old and dusty.
- The Lamp's bright light transitions into its weaker light in old age.

### 3. Keep the humans completely invisible

Communicate their presence through:
- Moving furniture.
- Doors opening and closing.
- Objects being relocated.
- Blankets moving.
- Drawings appearing.
- Toys disappearing.
- Sounds coming from outside the room.

This makes the objects the true protagonists.

### 4. Keep the emotional contrast

The first 22 seconds should have plenty of movement, humour, and personality. The middle should feel warmer and more nostalgic. The last 22 seconds should gradually become quieter and more emotional.

## Character roles and continuity notes

- **The Lamp:** The emotional protagonist. It experiences the room's entire life.
- **The Bird:** The secondary witness. It provides another perspective on loneliness, attachment, and memory.
- **The Teddy Bear:** Could be incorporated into the childhood and family scenes as a recurring background character.
- **The Cup:** Could appear in the family and later-life scenes, bringing small moments of humour and movement.

**Continuity note:** The original timeline introduces the Bird, although the initial character notes describe the Lamp, Teddy Bear, and Cup. The Bird has been kept here because it is central to the supplied storyboard. The Teddy Bear and Cup can be added as recurring characters without changing the eight-stage structure.

## Core creative direction

The strongest version of this film is not simply about a room changing over time. It is about the objects that stay, remember, and witness everything while the world around them changes.

**The humans live their lives. The objects live through them.**
